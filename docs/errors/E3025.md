# E3025 — a method takes all of `self`, defeating disjoint-field access

```ember,fails
struct P:
    pub x: i32
    pub y: i32

    fn set_x(mut self, v: i32):
        self.x = v

fn main():
    p = P(1, 2)
    a: ref mut i32 = ref mut p.x
    p.set_x(10)
    println(a)
```

```text
error[E3025]: cannot call a method on `p` while `p.x` is mutably borrowed
  --> page.em:18:5
   |
17 |     a: ref mut i32 = ref mut p.x
   |                      ----------- mutable borrow of `p.x` starts here
18 |     p.set_x(10)
   |     ^ conflicting access here
   |
   = help: inline the field access, take the two fields as separate parameters, or split the method
   = note: a method takes all of `self`, so disjoint fields do not stay disjoint across a call (BRW-4)
```

## Why

`ref mut p.x` and `ref mut p.y` may coexist — they are different fields, and
the borrow checker understands field projections. But a method call does not
take a field, it takes all of `self`: `p.set_x(10)` mutably borrows the whole
`p`, including the `x` already borrowed. The disjointness the field borrows
relied on is defeated by the call, not by a second borrow, so the fix is about
the call — end the borrow first, or do not route this access through a method.

This is worth its own code rather than an `E3022` because the advice differs.
`E3022` says two borrows collide; here there is one borrow and one call, and
"shorten its last use" may not even apply — the call is the thing that does
not fit, so the help names restructuring the call instead.

## The fix

Do not hold a field borrow across a method call on the same value. Either end
the borrow first:

```ember,fixed
struct P:
    pub x: i32
    pub y: i32

    fn set_x(mut self, v: i32):
        self.x = v

fn main():
    p = P(1, 2)
    p.set_x(10)
    a: ref mut i32 = ref mut p.x
    a = 20
    println(a + p.y)
```

or restructure the call itself — inline the field access, take the two fields
as separate parameters, or split the method — which is what the diagnostic's
help suggests.
